The lowest effective dose should be given to avoid the risk of hypothyroidism. When remission is observed, treatment should be withdrawn under close supervision.  The dose should not be doubled to take account of a missed dose.  Caution is advised when switching between different brands of propylthiouracil given the lack of comparability data.  Dosage in adults, including the elderly  Initially 150 to 300 mg per day in divided doses (typically every 8 hours) up to 500 mg per day, until the patient becomes euthyroid.  When the condition is controlled (usually after 1-2 months), the dose can be reduced to 50 to 150 mg daily. Treatment should be for as short a duration as possible.  Dose selection should proceed with caution in the elderly due to the greater incidence of decreased renal or hepatic function and concomitant medical conditions.

Haematological toxicity and agranulocytosis  Agranulocytosis is a potentially life-threatening side effect of propylthiouracil. It typically occurs during the first months of therapy.  Particular care should be exercised with patients who are receiving concomitant drugs known to be associated with agranulocytosis.  Due to the risk of   agranulocytosis   it is advised that patients should be warned to report to their doctor in the event of a sore throat, fever, mouth ulcers, bruising, malaise, non-specific illness or other symptoms of infection immediately.  A full blood count should be performed and treatment should be discontinued immediately if there is clinical or laboratory evidence of   neutropaenia .  Halycil can adversely affect   prothrombin and platelets : both full blood count and coagulation profile need to be   monitored.  Hepatotoxicity  Some cases of severe hepatic reactions, both in adults and children, including fatal cases and cases requiring a liver transplant, have been reported with propylthiouracil. Time to onset varies but in a majority of cases the liver reaction occurs within six months.  If significant hepatic enzyme abnormalities develop during treatment with Halycil, this medicine should be discontinued immediately (see Section 4.8).  Halycil must be used with caution in patients with hepatic disease (see Section 4.2). Patients should be advised of the symptoms of hepatic dysfunction (anorexia, pruritus, right upper quadrant pain, etc.) and told to report them immediately. The occurrence of hepatic necrosis may have fatal consequences (see Section 4.8).

Propylthiouracil-induced ANCA- associated vasculitis (AAV) is characterised by multiple antigenicity and presents similarly to other forms of drug-induced vasculitis. Intolerance to sugars  Patients with rare hereditary problems of galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency, or glucose- galactose malabsorption, should not take this medicine. Paediatric use  Halycil must not be used in children due to the risk of liver failure.  4.5   INTERACTIONS WITH OTHER MEDICINES AND OTHER FORMS OF INTERACTIONS  Theophylline, digoxin and beta-blockers  Drug induced changes in thyroid status may affect the dosage requirements for theophylline, digoxin or beta-blockers. The doses of theophylline, digoxin or beta-blockers may need to be reduced as thyroid function returns to normal.  Radio-iodine ( 131 I) therapy  Halycil should be withdrawn two to four days prior to radio-iodine ( 131 I) therapy. The dosage of radio-iodine may need to be adjusted because propylthiouracil may have a radioprotective effect and reduce the effectiveness of radio-iodine ( 131 I) therapy for hyperthyroidism. Treatment with Halycil can result in rapid normalisation in fertility once the thyroid function is controlled.  In males, hyperthyroidism can cause a marked reduction in sperm count resulting in infertility. Treatment with Halycil may result in normalisation in sperm count once the thyroid function is controlled.  Use in pregnancy  Propylthiouracil is classified as Pregnancy Category D - Drugs which have caused, are suspected to have caused, or may be expected to cause an increased incidence of human foetal malformations or irreversible damage. These drugs may also have adverse pharmacological effects.  Individual benefit / risk assessment is necessary before starting treatment with Halycil during pregnancy. Hyperthyroidism in pregnant women should be adequately treated to prevent serious maternal and foetal complications.  Halycil may be given in pregnancy:  •   the drug may be used prior to conception and in the first trimester of pregnancy, where clinically appropriate, due to the higher risk of congenital abnormalities with other antithyroid drugs, such as methimazole, during foetal organogenesis in the first trimester.  •   after the first trimester of pregnancy, the use of an alternative antithyroid medication may be advisable given the potential for maternal hepatotoxicity from propylthiouracil.  •   if the patient becomes pregnant while taking propylthiouracil, the patient should be warned of the potential for liver damage to the mother and foetus.  •   close maternal, foetal and neonatal monitoring is always necessary: maternal hypothyroidism should be avoided as it increases the risk of birth defects.  Propylthiouracil   crosses the placenta and in high doses may cause foetal goitre and hypothyroidism, therefore the lowest possible dose should be given and thyroid function should be monitored every four to six weeks to maintain optimum control.  Cases of congenital anomalies have been observed post-marketing and epidemiological studies suggest an increased risk of congenital malformations.  There are insufficient animal studies available regarding reproductive and developmental toxicity with propylthiouracil in the context of restoring normal thyroid function to inform risk assessment.  Use in lactation  Propylthiouracil transfers to breast milk, reaching about 10% of the serum concentration.  Neonatal development and infant thyroid function should be closely monitored.

It allows continued monitoring of the benefit-risk balance of the medicinal product. Healthcare professionals are asked to report any suspected adverse reactions at   www.tga.gov.au/reporting-  problems .  4.9   OVERDOSE  Symptoms  Goitre and hypothyroidism may be induced by repeated over dosage. Overdose may manifest as vomiting, epigastric distress, headache, fever, arthralgia, pruritus, pancytopenia and agranulocytosis.  Management  The treatment of propylthiouracil overdose should aim to minimise the amount of drug absorbed in the circulation. Treatment should involve liberal use of oral fluids.  Activated charcoal may reduce absorption of propylthiouracil if given within one or two hours after ingestion. In patients who are not fully conscious or have impaired gag reflex, consideration should be given to administering activated charcoal via a nasogastric tube, once the airway is protected.  General symptomatic and supportive measures should then be instituted. A full blood analysis should be considered because of the slight risk of haematological complications and appropriate therapy given if bone marrow depression develops.  There is no specific antidote for propylthiouracil.   For information on the management of overdose, contact the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26 (Australia).  5   PHARMACOLOGICAL PROPERTIES  5.1   PHARMACODYNAMIC PROPERTIES  Category: Antithyroid therapy.   ATC Code: H03BA02: Antithyroid preparations – Thiouracils.  Mechanism of action  Propylthiouracil is an antithyroid drug that inhibits the synthesis of the thyroid hormones thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3). This is achieved by interference with the incorporation of iodine into tyrosyl residues and the coupling of such residues to form iodothyronines, and is mediated by inhibition of the enzyme peroxidase. Propylthiouracil also acts to inhibit the peripheral conversion of T4 to the more active form T3, a secondary mode of action of importance in cases of severe thyrotoxicosis.  The effects of propylthiouracil are only manifested after a latent period of up to 3 to 4 weeks because all the preformed hormone must be used up before circulatory concentrations will fall.  Clinical   trials  No data are available.  5.2   PHARMACOKINETIC PROPERTIES  Absorption  Propylthiouracil is rapidly absorbed from the gut with average peak blood levels about one hour after administration of an oral dose. Between half and three quarters of the oral dose is bioavailable due to incomplete absorption or rapid first pass metabolism by the liver.

Version 01  Distribution  The plasma half-life is 1-3 hours, the volume of distribution approximately 30 L with about 80% plasma binding.  Propylthiouracil   crosses the placental barrier and is also found in breast milk.  Metabolism  The major metabolic pathway for propylthiouracil is glucuronidation via UGT1A9.  Excretion  Most of the drug is excreted as the glucuronic acid conjugate in the urine.  5.3   PRECLINICAL SAFETY DATA  Genotoxicity  Negative results were returned for propylthiouracil in assays for bacterial mutagenicity and for chromosomal aberrations   in vitro , conducted in the absence (but not presence) of metabolic activation. While these findings are consistent with an absence of genotoxicity, the available data are too limited to reach a definitive conclusion on the genotoxic potential of propylthiouracil.  Carcin ogenicity  Thyroid carcinogenicity has been demonstrated with propylthiouracil in mice, rats, hamsters and guinea pigs. Such tumours occurred in the context of thyroid hyperplasia / increased thyroid stimulating hormone levels, with inhibition of thyroid hormone production driving thyroid cell proliferation as a compensatory response. The relevance of the animal thyroid tumour findings to patients appears limited considering therapy in humans is to normalise thyroid hormone levels rather than to induce continuous suppression of thyroid function, as in animals.  6   PHARMACEUTICAL PARTICULARS  6.1   LIST OF EXCIPIENTS  Lactose monohydrate  Acacia, spray-dried  Croscarmellose sodium  Sodium lauryl sulfate  Magnesium stearate  6.2   INCOMPATIBILITIES  Incompatibilities were either not assessed or not identified as part of the registration of this medicine.  6.3   SHELF LIFE  4 years.  6.4   SPECIAL PRECAUTIONS FOR STORAGE  Store below 30°C.
