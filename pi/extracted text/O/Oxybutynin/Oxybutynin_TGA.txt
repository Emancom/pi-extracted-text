Avoid dosage in high environmental temperatures and excessive exercise in high temperatures since oxybutynin hydrochloride administered under these conditions can cause heat prostration (fever and heat stroke due to decreased sweating).  In an environment where perspiration occurs, the sweating inhibitory action may increase body temperature. Patients should be instructed to take appropriate measures in the event of symptoms indicative of heat illness.  The anticholinergic effect of oxybutynin hydrochloride inhibits the contraction and movement of gastrointestinal smooth muscles, potentially leading to gastrointestinal hypomotility. Patients should be instructed to discontinue APOHIDE ®   and seek medical attention if they experience gastrointestinal symptoms.  APOHIDE ®   should not be applied to skin areas with wounds, eczema, dermatitis or similar conditions. Doing so may increase the absorption into the body, potentially leading to adverse effects based on its anticholinergic effects.  Due to the anticholinergic effect, which may induce urinary retention, care should be taken when using APOHIDE ®   in patients with complicated lower urinary tract obstruction diseases (e.g., prostatic hyperplasia) (excluding patients with impaired urination).  Due to the anticholinergic effect, which may worsen sympathomimetic symptoms such as tachycardia, care should be taken when using APOHIDE ®   in patients with hyperthyroidism.  Enhanced compensatory sympathetic nervous system may be further enhanced, care should be taken when using APOHIDE ®   in patients with congestive cardiac failure.  In patients with tachyarrhythmia, the parasympatholytic effect may cause dominance of sympathetic nerves, resulting in increased heart rate, care should be taken when using APOHIDE ®   in patients with arrhythmia.  Megacolon toxicity may occur, care should be taken when using APOHIDE ®   in patients with ulcerative colitis.  Symptoms may worsen or neuropsychiatric symptoms may occur, care should be taken when using APOHIDE ®   in patients with Parkinson’s disease or cerebrovascular disorder.  Due to the anticholinergic effect, which may worsen symptoms, care should be taken when using APOHIDE ®   in patients with dementia or cognitive impairment.

APOHIDE PI v1.0   Page   4   of   11  Precautions concerning use  After applying APOHIDE ®   to the palms before bedtime, it is essential to refrain from wetting the hands to prevent unintentional removal of the product. Following waking up, it is important to avoid contact between the treated hands with non-application areas (e.g., eyes) until thorough handwashing has occurred. In the event that the lotion enters the eyes during application, the eyes must immediately be rinsed with water. After APOHIDE ®   application, it is advised to not cover the palms with occlusive dressings, such as airtight gloves.  APOHIDE ®   must be kept out of the reach of children to prevent contact with the product.  APOHIDE ®   contains alcohol, a flammable substance. It should be stored and used away from fire, flame, or heat.  4.5   I NTERACTIONS WITH OTHER MEDICINES AND OTHER FORMS OF INTERACTIONS  Oxybutynin is metabolised by CYP3A4, a hepatic enzyme responsible for drug metabolism. Concomitant administration with a CYP3A4 inhibitor can strongly inhibit oxybutynin metabolism and increase oxybutynin exposure. The anticholinergic effect of oxybutynin is enhanced by its concomitant use with other agents with anticholinergic properties and care should be taken if APOHIDE ®   is used concurrently with such drugs. These include the tricyclic antidepressants phenothiazines and monoamine oxidase inhibitors.  Concomitant use of APOHIDE ®   with cholinesterase inhibitors may result in reduced cholinesterase inhibitor efficacy due to its anticholinergic properties.  4.6   F ERTILITY ,   PREGNANCY AND LACTATION  Effects on fertility  No data available   for APOHIDE ® . Reproduction studies with oxybutynin hydrochloride in rats showed no definite evidence of impaired fertility.  Use in pregnancy – Pregnancy Category B1  No data available for APOHIDE ® . Animal studies with other oxybutynin products showed no clear evidence of teratogenicity or other embryotoxic effects in rats and rabbits at oral doses up to 160 and 100 mg/kg/day respectively. However, the incidence of abortion was slightly increased at the highest dose level in rabbits.  The safety of oxybutynin hydrochloride in women who are or who may become pregnant has not been established, it should be given only when the potential benefits outweigh the possible hazards.  Use in lactation  No data available for APOHIDE ® . Animal studies with other oxybutynin products showed that oxybutynin or its metabolites are excreted in milk. It is not known whether oxybutynin is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised when APOHIDE ®   is administered to a nursing woman.

Treatment  Patients should be advised to cease using APOHIDE ®   and seek immediate medical attention if any of the aforementioned symptoms manifest. Treatment tailored to each specific symptom should be promptly administered.  For information on the management of an overdose, contact the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26 (Australia).  5   PHARMACOLOGICAL PROPERTIES  5.1   P HARMACODYNAMIC PROPERTIES  Mechanism of action  Oxybutynin hydrochloride has anticholinergic properties that may enable it to antagonise the muscarinic receptor expressed in the eccrine sweat glands, acting as an antiperspirant.  Affinity to Muscarinic Receptors  In a binding study using human muscarinic receptors (M 1 , M 2 , M 3 , M 4   and M 5 ), oxybutynin inhibited [ 3 H] N -methylscopolamine binding in a competitive manner, and showed high affinity for muscarinic M 3   and M 4   receptors ( in vitro ).  Anticholinergic Effect  In studies using isolated tissues from rats, guinea pigs and humans, oxybutynin suppressed bladder contraction induced by carbachol or such and decrease in heart rate induced by carbachol ( in vitro ).  Clinical trials  The efficacy and safety of APOHIDE ®   were evaluated in patients with primary palmar hyperhidrosis in two Phase 3 studies.
