Smoking is an important risk factor for the development of emphysema in patients with Alpha -PI deficiency.(1,2) Because emphysema affects many, but not all individuals with the more severe genetic variants of Alpha -PI deficiency, augmentation therapy with Alpha -PI is indicated only in patients with severe Alpha -PI deficiency who have clinically evident emphysema. Only some Alpha -PI alleles are associated with clinically apparent Alpha -PI deficiency.(3,4) Approximately 95% of all severely deficient patients are homozygous for the PiZ allele.(4) Individuals with the PiZZ variant typically have serum Alpha -PI levels less than 35% of the average normal level. Individuals with the Pi(null)(null) variant have undetectable Alpha -PI protein in their serum. Individuals with these low serum Alpha -PI levels, i.e., less than 11 μM, have a markedly increased risk for developing emphysema over their lifetimes. In addition, PiSZ individuals, whose serum Alpha -PI levels range from approximately 9 to 23 μM,(5) are considered to have moderately increased risk for developing emphysema, regardless of whether their serum Alpha -PI levels are above or below 11 μM. Augmenting the levels of functional protease inhibitor by intravenous infusion is an approach to therapy for patients with Alpha -PI deficiency. The intended theoretical goal is to provide protection to the lower respiratory tract by correcting the imbalance between neutrophil elastase and protease inhibitors. Whether augmentation therapy with any Alpha -PI product actually protects the lower respiratory tract from progressive emphysematous changes has not been demonstrated in adequately powered, randomized controlled, clinical trials. Although the maintenance of blood serum levels of Alpha -PI (antigenically measured) above 11 μM has been historically postulated to provide therapeutically relevant anti-neutrophil elastase protection,(6) this has not been proven. Individuals with severe Alpha -PI deficiency have been shown to have increased neutrophil and neutrophil elastase concentrations in lung epithelial lining fluid compared to normal PiMM individuals, and some PiSZ individuals with Alpha -PI above 11 μM have emphysema attributed to Alpha -PI deficiency. These observations underscore the uncertainty regarding the appropriate therapeutic target serum level of Alpha -PI during augmentation therapy. The pathogenesis of emphysema is understood as described in the “protease- antiprotease imbalance” model. Alpha -PI is understood to be the primary antiprotease in the lower respiratory tract, where it inhibits neutrophil elastase (NE). Study subjects were randomly assigned to receive either 60 mg/kg body weight of functional PROLASTIN-C LIQUID or PROLASTIN-C weekly by intravenous infusion during the first 8-week treatment period.
